# Praca domowa — W sklepie

Homework for the lesson **Gdzie jest pies?** (2026-08-16).
Student sheet: `Downloads/flashcards/lessons/2026-08-18-homework-w-sklepie.html`

A story with dialogue inside it, 109 words, same shape as the lesson's own.
Every lemma is in the deck; `mój` and `ja`/`my`/`ty` are grammar words he has
already met.

## Why the gaps carry no verb cue

The twelve `kind: "form"` conjugation drills go live in his next session and
arrive at five a day, so within three days the deck is drilling *jestem /
jesteś / jest / …* and *mam / masz / ma / …* every morning. Those cards name
the verb in brackets — `Dziecko ___ (być) w parku` — so they test the
**form** and never the **choice**.

This sheet therefore gives no cue. He has to decide `być` or `mieć` first,
and only then the person. That is the one thing the cards structurally
cannot ask him, and it is the distinction he actually gets wrong.

The paradigm table is still on the sheet. With the verb uncued, leaning on
it costs him nothing worth having — recall of the cells is what the
flashcards are for.

## Story

Rano dziecko idzie do sklepu. Pies **(1)** w domu. Pies czeka.

Sklep **(2)** nowy. W sklepie stoi stara kobieta. Kobieta **(3)** chleb,
mleko i kaszę.

— Cześć! Czy ty **(4)** wodę? — mówi dziecko.
— Nie, ja nie **(5)** wody. Ale **(6)** mleko — mówi kobieta. — Czy ty
**(7)** czas?
— Tak, **(8)** czas.

Kobieta patrzy na dziecko.

— Kto **(9)** w domu? — mówi kobieta.
— Mój pies. **(10)** stary i dziwny. Pies nie **(11)** piłki.
— Ptaki **(12)** na drzewie — mówi kobieta. — Śpiewają. To **(13)** dobre
miejsce.

Dziecko kupuje chleb i mleko. Potem wraca drogą do domu.

Pies czeka. Dziecko daje psu chleb.

— Teraz my **(14)** chleb! Ty **(15)** dobry — mówi dziecko. — My **(16)**
w domu.

## Answers

| | answer | why only this one |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | jest | locative `w domu`, no object — `mieć` is impossible |
| 2 | jest | adjective complement |
| 3 | ma | three accusative objects |
| 4 | masz | explicit `ty`; accusative `wodę` |
| 5 | mam | explicit `ja`; genitive `wody` after `nie` |
| 6 | mam | same speaker, same person, accusative `mleko` |
| 7 | masz | explicit `ty` |
| 8 | mam | the answer to a `ty` question about oneself |
| 9 | jest | `kto` takes a singular verb; locative |
| 10 | Jest | subject is `mój pies`, singular, adjective complement |
| 11 | ma | explicit subject `pies`; genitive `piłki` after `nie` |
| 12 | są | plural subject, locative |
| 13 | jest | `to ___` + noun phrase |
| 14 | mamy | explicit `my`; accusative `chleb` |
| 15 | jesteś | explicit `ty`; adjective complement |
| 16 | jesteśmy | explicit `my`; locative |

Eight `być`, eight `mieć` — deliberately even, so he cannot get a run right
by guessing that one verb is more common than the other.

Forms covered: `jest` ×5, `są`, `jesteś`, `jesteśmy`; `mam` ×3, `masz` ×2,
`ma` ×2, `mamy`. Not covered: `jesteście`, `macie`, `mają` — 2nd person
plural needs a group to address, which a two-hander cannot supply. The
flashcards cover all six persons of both verbs, so nothing is lost.

## How each gap was made unambiguous

Polish drops pronouns, so a bare gap usually admits several persons —
`___ w parku` is equally *jestem*, *jesteś* or *jest*. Two devices fix that:

- **A noun subject** for third person (1, 2, 3, 11, 12) — nothing else fits.
- **An explicit pronoun** where the person would otherwise float
  (4, 5, 7, 14, 15, 16). `ja nie mam` is emphatic rather than neutral, but
  it is ordinary Polish in a contrast, and here it earns its place.

Gaps 6, 8 and 10 carry no marker and rely on the sentence before: 6 and 8
continue a speaker already established in the first person, and 10's subject
is `mój pies` from the previous line. Those three are the ones to watch when
you go over it — if he misses one, it is discourse tracking rather than the
verb.

## What to look at

**Gap 9 is the `kto` test.** You have mentioned in a lesson that `kto` takes
a singular verb, so this is a check rather than an ambush. *Kto są w domu?*
means he heard it and has not absorbed it.

**Gaps 5 and 11 are the genitive pair.** The nouns are already written in
(`wody`, `piłki`), so he only supplies the verb — but they are the two places
where he can see that `nie` changed the ending, without being asked to
produce it yet.

**A wrong verb matters more than a wrong person.** *Pies ma w domu* is a
different kind of error from *Pies są w domu*: the first says he has not
separated the two verbs, the second is an agreement slip. Only the first is
worth a lesson.
