# Lekcja 2026-09-08 — jak często, i chcieć

Sheets: `Downloads/flashcards/lessons/2026-09-08-czytanie.html` (the story)
and `2026-09-08-sybilanty.html` (the spoken sibilant list).

## Why these words

The six frequency adverbs were carded together on 2026-08-29 and prioritised
together, so they all arrived within days of each other. Their card histories
are the argument for this lesson:

| word | history | ease |
| --- | --- | --- |
| często | `xxxxxxvx` | 1.30, the floor |
| czasami | `xxxvxv` | 1.70 |
| zawsze | `xxxxvv` | 1.70 |
| codziennie | `xv` | 2.30 |
| zwykle, nigdy | one rep each | 2.50 |

Words introduced the same week that are *not* a set — lubić, ulica, karta,
samochód — are clean `vvv` at 2.50. So this is not difficulty, it is
interference: six near-synonyms competing, each defined only by a gloss that
reads "how often".

That was my error in proposing them as a set and flagging them all priority.
Worth remembering before carding another semantic cluster in one go.

`chcieć` is here too because it is his single worst card in the deck — missed
17 of 36 — and because it costs nothing to carry: every frequency sentence
wants a verb, and this is the one that needs the exposure.

## How the story is staged

Not all six at once, which would repeat the mistake.

1. **zawsze and nigdy** open it — maximally separated, and `nigdy` brings its
   own hook, the double negative.
2. **codziennie** next, concrete and built on `dzień`, which he knows.
3. **często, czasami and zwykle** only in the middle section, where the
   contrast is the point rather than incidental.
4. The closing line puts four of them in a row deliberately, as a summary he
   has already been walked through rather than a cold test.

## Story

Marek zawsze pije kawę rano. Nigdy nie pije mleka.

— Chcesz mleko? — pyta kobieta w sklepie.
— Nie. Nigdy nie chcę mleka. Zawsze chcę kawę.

Marek codziennie chodzi do pracy. Praca jest w mieście. Codziennie widzi miasto, ulice i ludzi.

W pracy Marek często je chleb. Czasami je kaszę. Zwykle pije wodę.

— Jak często chodzisz do parku? — pyta kobieta.
— Czasami. Zwykle jestem w pracy. Ale zawsze chcę iść!

Wieczorem Marek zwykle wraca do domu. Czasami spotyka ludzi na ulicy. Często chce mówić, ale nie ma czasu.

Teraz Marek siedzi w domu i myśli:

— Zawsze chcę jeść. Często chcę iść do parku. Czasami chcę śpiewać. Nigdy nie chcę czekać!

## What to watch

**często against czasami is the real pair.** They are his two worst and they
also sound alike — `czę` and `cza`. Worth testing whether he can hear them
apart at all before asking what they mean; if not, that is a listening
problem wearing a vocabulary problem's clothes.

**`chcę` versus `chcieć`.** He almost certainly knows `chcę`. If he does not
recognise the infinitive, that is the same gap `być` and `mieć` needed
`kind: "form"` cards to close, and it would explain a card that has resisted
a new picture and new audio.
