## Why this, and why this group first

The first of three present-tense lessons, one conjugation group each. The
case for verbs before cases came from his own trouble list, which on
2026-09-13 was mostly verbs whose dictionary form he never says: the cards
show `chcieć`, `wiedzieć`, `pamiętać`, `czytać` and `znać`, and he says
*chcę*, *wiem*, *pamiętam*, *czytam* and *znam*. It is the gap `być` and
`mieć` had, spread across every verb. And because Polish drops the pronoun,
the ending is the only thing that says who is doing it — every card sentence
is written in 1st and 2nd person on purpose, and until now he has been reading
those forms without the key.

The -am group goes first because it is fully regular and he already owns the
model: *mam, masz, ma, mamy, macie, mają* is this exact pattern, which is why
`mieć` sits beside `czekać` in the table. It covers ten verbs he has met,
three of them on the trouble list.

Next: -ę / -isz (*lubię, robię, mówię, widzę, chodzę, płacę, muszę, myślę*),
then -ę / -esz (*chcę, mogę, idę, jadę, piję*) last because the stems move.
One group per lesson, for the same reason the deck does not card a semantic
set at once. The accusative (*-a → -ę*: *chcę kawę, czytam książkę*) is the
lesson after the verbs, on its own.

**No conjugation cards.** The `być`/`mieć` form cards are a bounded exception
and CLAUDE.md says not to let it grow; this lives in lessons and homework.

## Story

Sven czeka na pociąg. Ale pociągu nie ma.

Sven siada na ławce i czyta książkę.

Ewa też czeka na pociąg.

— Cześć, Sven! Co czytasz? — pyta Ewa.

— Polską książkę. A ty? Też czekasz?

— Tak, czekam. Wracam do domu.

— Gdzie mieszkasz? — pyta Sven.

— W małym mieście. Nie pamiętasz?

— Nie, nie pamiętam.

Ludzie czekają. Małe dziecko patrzy na ludzi.

— Czekacie na pociąg? — pyta dziecko.

— Tak, czekamy — mówi Ewa. — A ty?

— Ja szukam psa. Mam małego psa. Znacie mojego psa?

— Nie znamy — mówi Sven. — Ale szukamy!

— Jak ma na imię twój pies? — pyta Ewa.

— Kot!

Sven i Ewa szukają psa. Ludzie też szukają.

— Kot! Kot! — mówią ludzie.

Sven patrzy pod ławkę. Tam jest mały pies! Pies ma czerwoną piłkę.

Dziecko śpiewa: — Mój Kot! Mój Kot!

Pociąg jedzie.

Sven, Ewa, dziecko i pies siadają w pociągu.

— Wracacie do domu? — pyta dziecko.

— Tak, wracamy — mówi Ewa.

— Ja też! I Kot też!

### How it is built

- **All ten -am verbs he has met are in it**: czekać, czytać, mieszkać,
  pamiętać, pytać, siadać, szukać, śpiewać, wracać, znać.
- **`czekać` appears in all six persons** — *czekam, czekasz, czeka, czekamy,
  czekacie, czekają* — so one whole paradigm is there to point at, next to
  the `mieć` column he already knows.
- **Other conjugation groups kept to what the scenes need**: *patrzy, mówi,
  jedzie, jest*. Nothing competes with the pattern.
- **The plurals arrive for a reason.** A child talking to two adults gives
  *wy*, their answers give *my*, a crowd searching gives *oni*.
- **The dog is called Kot.** A joke he can get, and a crowd shouting *Kot!
  Kot!* for a dog is a hook. `Kot` maps to `kot` in the lemma block rather
  than `-`: the joke only works because he knows the word, so it should be
  checked like one.
- Five cards that are going wrong get context: `pociąg` (five times),
  `czytać`, `książka`, `pamiętać`, `znać`. No new words at all.

On the sheet every -am form is highlighted with only its **ending**
coloured — czek**am** — so the stem-plus-ending split is visible rather
than described. `mieć` is not highlighted in the story; it is the model in
the table, not the subject.

## Exercise — answers

| | item | answer | person | what decides it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Dziecko ___ (mieszkać) w małym mieście. | **mieszka** | on | noun subject |
| 2 | — Znasz Ewę? — Tak, ___ (znać)! | **znam** | ja | reply to ty |
| 3 | Sven i Ewa ___ (znać) dobre miejsce. | **znają** | oni | two-person subject |
| 4 | — Sven, Adam, ___ (pytać) Ewę? — Tak. | **pytacie** | wy | speaking to two people |
| 5 | Sven i ja ___ (czytać) polskie książki. | **czytamy** | my | “X i ja” is my |
| 6 | — Ewa, ___ (wracać) do domu? — Tak, wracam. | **wracasz** | ty | speaking to one person |
| 7 | Ludzie w pociągu ___ (czytać). | **czytają** | oni | noun subject |
| 8 | Pies nie ___ (pamiętać), gdzie jest piłka. | **pamięta** | on | noun subject |
| 9 | — Czy wy ___ (śpiewać), dzieci? | **śpiewacie** | wy | pronoun |
| 10 | — Siadacie? — Tak, ___ (siadać) — mówią Sven i Ewa. | **siadamy** | my | two people answer together |
| 11 | — Sven, czego ___ (szukać)? — Mojej książki. | **szukasz** | ty | speaking to one person |
| 12 | Dziecko i pies ___ (wracać) do domu. | **wracają** | oni | two-person subject |
| 13 | — Gdzie wy ___ (mieszkać)? — pyta Sven. | **mieszkacie** | wy | pronoun |
| 14 | — Co robisz rano? — ___ (śpiewać)! | **śpiewam** | ja | reply to ty |

Two per person for *ja, ty, on, my*; three each for *wy* and *oni*, because
*-acie* and *-ają* are the endings he has not been saying.

### The rules it was built to

These are checked, not intended:

- **Exactly one person fits each item.** Polish drops the pronoun, so a gap
  with nothing to decide it — `___ (czekać) na pociąg` — has six right
  answers. That is the failure of the first frequency sheet in a new form.
  Every item carries one of: a stated pronoun, a noun subject, a two-person
  subject, direct address, or a question/answer pair that fixes the person.
- **No answer appears anywhere in the story on the same page.** Checked
  mechanically. That is why `czekać` — all six forms already in the story — is
  never gapped, and why no *siadają*, *szukamy* or *wracacie* appear.
- **No answer is printed in another item's clue.** Also checked: item 6 says
  *wracam* but no item's answer is *wracam*.
- **`mieć` is never gapped**, because *mieć → mam* changes its stem and is not
  mechanical from the infinitive.

## What to watch

**Items 5 and 10 are the traps.** *Sven i ja* looks like it wants *-ają*
because there are two names' worth of people, and it is *czytamy*. Item 10 is
two people answering a *wy* question, which flips it to *my*. Getting the
compound-subject items right (3, 12) and these two wrong means he is counting
people rather than asking who is speaking.

**Item 4 is address, not description.** *Sven, Adam, ___ Ewę?* is spoken to
them, so *pytacie*; *pytają* would mean he read the names as the subject.

**The *-ają* ending.** It is the least regular-looking of the six — not
*-amą*, not *-ami* — and it will be the one he writes wrong if any.
