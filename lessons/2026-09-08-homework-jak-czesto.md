# Praca domowa — Jak często

Homework set 2026-09-08, after [[2026-09-08-zawsze-nigdy]]. Student sheet:
`Downloads/flashcards/lessons/2026-09-08-homework-jak-czesto.html` — two
parts, no answers on it.

## What it drills

**Part 1 — the four frequency words we kept.** `zawsze`, `nigdy`,
`codziennie`, `czasami`. `często` and `zwykle` are withdrawn from rotation
(see `tools/withdraw.py`) and deliberately do not appear anywhere on the
sheet: putting them back in front of him in writing while they are out of the
deck would rebuild exactly the six-way competition the withdrawal exists to
break.

Ten items, three options each, and **the setup line decides the answer** —
that is the whole design. A gap with three frequency adverbs in it is
otherwise a coin toss, because all three are grammatical almost everywhere.
So each item is two sentences: the first states a fact, the second has the
gap, and only one adverb is consistent with the fact.

Three of the ten also carry `nie`, which is the second thing being taught:
`nigdy` is never alone, it is always `nigdy nie`.

**Part 2 — a story on his weakest cards, with six new words.** The story is
built to put his worst cards in front of him in context rather than on a
flashcard: `chcieć` (missed 17 of 37, his worst), `wiedzieć` (7 of 19),
`dziwny`, `pamiętać`, `myśleć`, `móc`, `miejsce`, `droga`, `patrzeć`,
`pytać`, `pierwszy`, `skakać`, `chodzić`.

## The six new words

`czytać`, `książka`, `znać`, `pytanie`, `robić`, `słowo` — all already carded
and complete, all sitting unseen in the bank, all now `priority: true`, so
they arrive within days of him reading this. Glossed on the sheet in English
and Dutch.

They were picked to earn their place in *this* story rather than off a list:

- **`robić`** is the verb the follow-up exercise needs. *Co robisz rano? Co
  robisz wieczorem?* cannot be asked without it, and it is top-50 frequency.
- **`znać` against `wiedzieć`** is deliberate and is the one judgement call
  here. `wiedzieć` is one of his worst cards, and its note already cites
  Dutch *kennen*/*weten* — but a note on a card is read once and forgotten.
  The story uses them side by side eight times: *Nie wiem, co to jest* /
  *Nie znam tego słowa*, *Ewa zna polski* / *Marek nie wie, gdzie jest sklep*.
  This is two of a pair, not a set of six, and the extremes-first rule is
  satisfied — the two are as far apart as the distinction goes.
- **`czytać` and `książka`** collocate, and they are what he is actually
  doing when he reads this sheet.
- **`pytanie`** shares a root with `pytać`, which is a weak card (`xxvxvxv`).
- **`słowo`** is what the whole story is about.

## Part 1 — answers

| | answer | why only this one |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | nigdy | fish do not go to shops at any frequency; `nie` also forces it |
| 2 | zawsze | he has no money at all, so the card is not sometimes, it is every time |
| 3 | codziennie | *rano i wieczorem — zawsze!* is a daily rhythm, and `codziennie` is the one that counts days |
| 4 | nigdy | the sun is yellow, so never green; `nie` again |
| 5 | czasami | *ale nie zawsze* rules out both `zawsze` and `codziennie` by name |
| 6 | zawsze | three occasions listed, not three days — `codziennie` would be the wrong axis |
| 7 | czasami | *ale nie zawsze pociągiem* — the same move as 5, on a different word |
| 8 | codziennie | *nowy dzień, nowa kawa* is explicitly per day |
| 9 | nigdy | he dislikes milk, and `nie` is in the sentence |
| 10 | zawsze | he does not know where the shop is, so every time, not some times |

Three `nigdy`, three `zawsze`, two `codziennie`, two `czasami`, and the
correct option sits in a different position each time.

## What to watch

**Items 6 and 8 are the real test.** They are the `zawsze`/`codziennie`
distinction, which is the only pair on the sheet that is genuinely hard,
because English *always* covers both. `codziennie` counts days; `zawsze`
counts occasions. Six lists occasions (morning, at work, evening); eight
names a day. If he swaps them, that is the one worth ten minutes in the
lesson.

**Items 5 and 7 give the answer away if he reads carefully** — *ale nie
zawsze* is a signpost. That is intentional for two of ten: they are the ones
that reward reading the setup rather than guessing the gap, and they teach
the habit the other eight need.

**`znać` vs `wiedzieć` in the story.** Watch for him producing *wiem* where
`znam` belongs. Dutch gives him the distinction free — *ik ken dat woord
niet* / *ik weet het niet* — so if it goes wrong, the problem is that he has
not connected the Polish pair to the Dutch pair he already owns, which is a
thirty-second fix rather than a vocabulary problem.
